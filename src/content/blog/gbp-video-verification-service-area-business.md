<p>You're setting up a Google Business Profile for your service business and get through all the steps just fine — until you come to the verification. Now you only see one option: video verification. If you run into this, you aren't alone. Google Business Profiles are essential for <a href="/">digital marketing</a>, but getting them verified and live can be tricky. </p>
<p>Video verification is now usually the only method of Google Business Profile verification available, and it can feel especially confusing when you don't have a storefront to show. While it might take a little extra work, it isn't difficult as long as you're prepared. Here's what you need to know in 2026.</p>
<h2>Choose Your Business Type Carefully Before You Do Anything Else</h2>
<p>Google's updated verification flow now front-loads a business classification question that shapes everything that follows — including what type of verification Google will require, whether your business address gets displayed publicly, and whether your profile passes compliance checks or gets flagged for suspension.</p>
<p>When you initiate verification, Google will ask: <i>"What option best describes your business?"</i> For most service area businesses operating from a home address, the correct answer is something along the lines of "My business travels to meet customers without a staffed physical location." Choosing the wrong option — for instance, selecting that you have a physical location customers can visit — can lead to your listing being suspended. And if you select that your business is online-only, you won’t be eligible for a GBP at all. Take an extra moment here to pick the option that honestly reflects how your business operates.</p>
<h2>Plan Ahead, But Don't Pre-Record</h2>
<p>You must record the video inside the verification flow on a mobile device, in one continuous take with no pauses or edits. Don't start the process until you know exactly what you'll show and have everything ready. You may need another person to help hold the camera, so plan for that ahead of time.</p>
<p>Google allows up to 5 minutes, but shorter is usually better; aim for 60 to 120 seconds. Shorter videos are easier for the review system to process and less likely to run into upload issues, but make sure you show everything you need to.</p>
<h2>Know What You Need to Show</h2>
<p>Your video needs to prove three things: that your business is located at the address you listed, that you have management access to that address, and that the business actually exists and belongs to you. To be successful, a verification video must prove location, that the business has the required equipment, and your ownership of the business.</p>
<p>Specifically, plan to show:</p>
<ul>
<li><b>Location:</b> Street signs, landmarks, or your house number that confirm your listed address</li>
<li><b>Access/management:</b> Use a key to unlock your front door — this simple step proves you control the location</li>
<li><b>Business equipment or branded materials:</b> Branded vehicles, tools, uniforms, business cards, or documents like a business license with your business name and address</li>
</ul>
<p>Do not show sensitive information like bank account numbers, tax information, identification numbers, other people's faces, or any other private information.</p>
<h2>Get It All in One Take</h2>
<p>This isn't meant to be a polished, professional video. The goal is one continuous, unedited shot that covers all three proof points. If your nearest street sign is a bit of a walk from your front door, practice the route before you hit record. Stay focused on getting everything you need within your 60–120 second target.</p>
<h2>Be Creative to Meet the Requirements</h2>
<p>Branded vehicles and equipment make it easy to prove your business exists, but don't panic if you don't have those. Show whatever branded materials you do have — a uniform, business cards, or official documents like a business license or utility bill that show your business name and address. Just make sure your business name and address match exactly across all documents and your Google profile. Even small mismatches, like a PO box on a utility bill when your physical location is different, can stop your verification.</p>
<h2>Don't Fake It</h2>
<p>Video verification exists because Google is cracking down on spam and fraudulent listings. Google now requires stronger proof, like video verification and supporting documents that match your profile, to help prevent spam, but this also makes verification harder for legitimate business owners. Don't create fake business signage or try to stage something that isn't real. If you genuinely can't prove your business exists, consider completing formal steps, like officially registering your business to obtain a license, or having permanent signage installed, before creating your profile.</p>
<h3>Example: Carpet Cleaning Business</h3>
<p>Let's say you operate a carpet cleaning business out of your home with a branded van. Your video might go like this:</p>
<p>Start at the nearest street sign or recognizable landmark. Pan or walk to your home (keep the camera rolling the whole time). Show your house number and use a key to unlock the front door. Walk to your branded van in the driveway, showing the business name on the outside. Unlock the van and clearly show your cleaning equipment inside, especially anything with your logo on it.</p>
<h3>Example: Cabinet Refinishing Business</h3>
<p>Let's say you refinish cabinets and operate out of your home, but you don't display your address publicly on your profile. Your video might go like this:</p>
<p>Start at the nearest street sign or a recognizable landmark close to your home. Walk or pan toward your house, keeping the camera rolling the entire time. Show your house number clearly. Use your key to unlock the front door (just a brief moment is enough to prove you have access to and manage the location). Now walk to your branded van parked in the driveway. Make sure the business name and logo on the van are clearly visible and in frame. Use your key to unlock the van. Open it up and show your refinishing equipment inside. If any of your supplies or equipment have your business name or logo on them, linger on those for a moment. If you have business cards, an invoice or bill, or another business document (without sensitive information) in the van, hold one up to the camera as additional proof that the business is real and operating.</p>
<h2>What to Do If It Doesn't Work</h2>
<p>If your video is rejected, don't just submit the same video again — figure out what was missing. Maybe you need a clearer shot of a street sign, or your branded equipment wasn't visible enough.</p>
<p>One important 2026 caveat: multiple failed verification attempts can now lead to a "No More Ways to Verify" message, a dead end that requires contacting Google support and waiting several business days before you can even try again. This makes it more important than ever to plan carefully before your first attempt rather than treating it as trial and error.</p>
