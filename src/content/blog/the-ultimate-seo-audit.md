<h3></h3>
<h3><b>GOOGLE MY BUSINESS AUDIT:</b></h3>
<p>Since Google Plus is such a power player in search results, and because Google searches now feature statistics from your Google My Business page, it’s important to make sure that your business is correctly listed with this service so that you can look your best when customers see your business in their search results. Follow these steps to incorporate Google Plus into your search engine strategy:</p>
<ol>
<li>Claim/Create your business - For a full explanation on how to claim your business, you can reference <a href="/remove-duplicate-google-plus-listing/">this post</a> about managing Google Plus listings. To create your business, log in to your personal Google Plus page, hover over the Home icon on the left of the feed, then click on Pages in the list. Click “Get Your Page”, then select your type of business. The next steps in the creation process involve searching for your business to make sure it isn’t already on Google Plus, and then finally creating and verifying the listing.</li>
<li>Make sure your page is completely optimized - This includes Name, Address, Contact Info, Categories, Hours, Photos, Introduction and adding a profile and cover photo, as well as following these additional guidelines:
<ul>
<li>If you are editing the address for a service area business, make sure that you check the box for “I deliver goods and services to customers at their location” and then uncheck the box that says “I also serve customers at my business address”. This will ensure that Google reads your My Business page correctly and also hides your address on your page.</li>
<li>Google doesn’t like answering service or 1-800 numbers. Use a local phone number whenever possible!!</li>
<li>Google also recommends using an email with a custom domain. This helps to establish validity with your page.</li>
<li>Choose two or three categories, and make sure they dictate what you ARE and not what you SELL. For example, select <b>Auto Repair Shop</b> instead of <b>Brake Repair &amp; Installation</b>.</li>
<li>Include as many photos of your business/products as you can, but at least 5 so the slots on the Maps results are all filled.</li>
<li>Introductions should be at least 250 words, and non-spammy.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Verify your page - This can be done over the phone or via postcard that arrives in 2-3 weeks at your business address. Most edits to your page and posts won’t go live until you are verified, so start this as early as you can.</li>
<li>Post quality content to your page - Check out <a href="/the-ten-best-social-media-strategies/">this post</a> on how to engage your followers and implement a good social media strategy.<strong><strong> </strong></strong></li>
</ol>

<h3><b>LOCAL LISTING AUDIT:</b></h3>
<p>A local listing or citation website is basically an index of businesses that allows users to search for specific industries or categories within a city. There are thousands of local listing sites out there, and they often feed off of each other, so make sure you’re sending out the correct information about your business:</p>
<ol>
<li>Start a Local Citations document or spreadsheet - keep track of all your local listings or citations and passwords as well as what needs to be taken care of for each.</li>
<li>Google yourself - start by looking for your top ranking business listings. Add each listing to your Local Citations spreadsheet and indicate whether you have access to it. Also mark any changes that need to be made to make each listing have consistent information about your business. Different pictures and descriptions are fine, but the Name, Address, and Phone Number of your business should be identical across the board.</li>
<li>Use a local listing aggregator or management system to find and remove duplicate and inaccurate listings - We wrote a post <a href="/the-top-five-local-listing-management-sites/">here</a> not too long ago about our favorite local listing management sites. Our recommended listing site is Moz Local because of its clean design and ability to find a lot of listings at once.</li>
<li>Use a local listing aggregator or management system to create listings -  you might be missing a mention on the major sites like Yelp, Bing, CitySearch, etc. Create as many listings as you can on the main aggregator sites, and over time, listings will be published out to even more sites.</li>
<li>Continue to use your Local Citations document - spend some time each month going over the spreadsheet and checking what still needs to be fixed. Local citations are important enough that they should be revisited often until any issues are solved, but they shouldn’t dominate your search engine optimization strategy.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong><strong> </strong></strong></p>
<h4><strong>POTENTIAL PROBLEMS:</strong></h4>
<ul>
<li>Listings that feed from another source, or can’t be edited</li>
<li>Changing your main business info after creating all these listings causes discrepancies and can hurt your rankings</li>
</ul>

<h3></h3>
<h3><b>CUSTOMER REVIEW STRATEGY AND ANALYSIS:</b></h3>
<p>Reviews are often ignored by business owners, but they are actually <a href="/how-customer-reviews-impact-search-rankings/">very important</a> to your online success. After all, who cares if you’re ranking first for every search for your keywords if all your reviews are one star? You’re going to get less business because online consumers can’t trust your business. Follow these steps to take control of your customer reviews and establish a good reputation for your business online:</p>
<ol>
<li>Decide where you would like to get more reviews. Your Google Plus page? Your Yelp page? Your website? Draft a strategy to acquire more reviews in these places. It would be a good idea to create a step-by-step guide that you can either email to customers or pass out directly after you complete your interactions. <strong>REMEMBER:</strong> Some review sites (Yelp especially) will penalize your business listing if it appears that your reviews are solicited or a reward is offered in exchange for the review. They want all reviews to be natural and from the heart of the reviewer, so keep this in mind as you draft your strategy.</li>
<li>Gain access to listings that currently have reviews for your business. This is easiest after you have finished your Local Listing Audit. Now that you know you can access and respond to reviews for these listing sites, start advertising them as additional places to leave a review for your service or as a page where potential customers can read more from people who have already experienced your product or service. We recommend creating a page on your website that features the icons for each of these listing sites linked to their respective business listing.</li>
<li>Attack negative reviews. If there is a way to delete a negative review for your business, do it. If not, move on to step 4.</li>
<li>Respond to all reviews. It’s easy to respond to people that are praising your work, but harder to know what to say to a disgruntled customer that just wants to rant about how you did them wrong. Respond anyways**. Decide what your company policy will be for people that had a bad experience (offer a redo of the service, a discount on their next service, a personal phone call, etc.) and then communicate with the reviewer. Sometimes it’s beneficial for your business listings to have negative reviews, as long as your response is also seen, because it shows researchers and potential customers that you care about the way your business is perceived and you’re willing to spend the time working things out with them. **Some people aren’t worth responding to. If it will only make matters worse, skip the negative review, but respond to as many as you can.</li>
<li>Keep checking back on these local listing sites, or set up notifications to alert you when you receive a new review.</li>
</ol>
<p> </p>
<h4><strong>POTENTIAL PROBLEMS:</strong></h4>
<ul>
<li>Negative reviews</li>
<li>No reviews</li>
<li>Reviews from customers that didn’t actually use your business, used your business before you were the owner, or used a similar business and confused your business listing with theirs</li>
</ul>

<h3><b>WEBSITE AUDIT:</b></h3>
<p>You may have a lot of website traffic, but how is your bounce rate? Are your rankings struggling too? There may be some key components of SEO that your website is missing:</p>
<ol>
<li>Add Alt Tags to all images.</li>
<li>Include a Page Title and Meta Description on every page.</li>
<li>Optimize your URLs (be careful of site errors that this may cause!)</li>
<li>Check for Site Errors like broken redirect links and server errors. There are a variety of resources online that can help you determine why your website might not be ranking as well as it should.</li>
<li>Improve your website content. Add paragraphs and revamp text. There should be <a href="/why-isnt-my-website-ranking/">no duplicate content</a>. Also, you should have many natural instances of your targeted keywords and related topics. This is where your previous understanding of <a href="/5-reasons-why-seo-is-crucial-for-new-businesses/">good SEO tactics</a> can really come into play.</li>
<li>Spend time <a href="/why-isnt-my-website-ranking/">optimizing your website</a> every month, because the fresher your content is, the more the search engines will trust it.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong><strong> </strong></strong></p>
<h4><strong>POTENTIAL PROBLEMS:</strong></h4>
<ul>
<li>Title Element/Meta Description too long/too short</li>
</ul>

<h3></h3>
<h3><b>SOCIAL AUDIT AND STRATEGY DEVELOPMENT:</b></h3>
<p>To really develop your brand, you can turn to social media. There are a variety of platforms on which to reach potential customers and build a following, so make sure you have a strategy in place by implementing these steps:<strong><strong> </strong></strong></p>
<ol>
<li>Set up your business page on Facebook, Foursquare, Google Plus, Twitter, Pinterest, Instagram, and YouTube, or as many of these sites as are applicable to your business. Make sure that your business information is correct on all.</li>
<li>Pay attention to special circumstances that apply to service area businesses. You may want to hide your physical location or create targeted boards, pages, or streams that cater to the geographic areas you serve.</li>
<li>Determine your company goals. Are you looking to gain brand awareness and exposure? Increase traffic to your physical location? Get more hits on your website? Develop a strategy that achieves these goals. Read <b><a href="/the-ten-best-social-media-strategies/">this post</a></b> to get some ideas of how you can use social media to benefit your business.</li>
<li>Post regularly and stay apprised of the activity on your social pages. Use these as unwarranted outlets to gauge customer satisfaction, research and development, and general clues as to how your business is doing.</li>
<li>Be sure to link all your social pages to your website and each other. Encourage website visitors to visit your business pages on social media and direct social followers to your website to build strong connections.</li>
<li>Include <a href="/how-to-post-better-images-online/">high-quality graphics</a> on all your social platforms.</li>
</ol>
