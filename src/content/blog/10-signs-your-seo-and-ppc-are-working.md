<p>Marketing reports are good at producing numbers. They're less good at telling you whether those numbers mean anything for your business.</p>
<p>The good news is that when <a href="/services/search-engine-optimization/">SEO</a> and <a href="/services/pay-per-click/">paid ads</a> are working, the evidence shows up in specific places you can check yourself.</p>
<p><b>Not every sign will appear at the same time.</b> SEO improvements can take months, while PPC changes can sometimes produce results much faster. Local visibility, branded searches, reviews, and website behavior may follow their own timelines. Look for several signals moving in the right direction rather than expecting every metric to improve every month.</p>
<p>Here are ten signs to look for, and where to find each one.</p>
<h2>1. More conversions</h2>
<p>This is the sign that matters most. Calls, form submissions, bookings, and purchases are the point of everything else on this list. You spend money on digital marketing in order to make money. However, it isn’t the only thing to look for as a sign your marketing is working.</p>
<p><b>Where to check:</b> Google Analytics (key events), your ad platform's conversion reports, and your call tracking or CRM. If conversions aren't being tracked yet, that's the first thing to fix.</p>
<h2>2. Better return on ad spend</h2>
<p>A healthy paid campaign should become more efficient as keywords, ads, targeting, and landing pages are refined. Over time, you may see lower costs per conversion or a stronger return from the same ad spend.</p>
<p><b>Where to check:</b> Google Ads or Microsoft Ads for metrics like cost per lead, cost per qualified lead, and ROAS where revenue data is available.</p>
<h2>3. Rankings are improving for the keywords that matter</h2>
<p>Notice the wording: the keywords that matter. Ranking for dozens of loosely related terms isn't much help. Climbing for the searches your ideal customers use when they're ready to buy is. These higher-value keywords may take longer to rank for, but improvement over time makes a big difference.</p>
<p><b>Where to check:</b> Google Search Console (Performance report, filtered by query) and a rank tracker. Rankings vary by location and device, so look at trends rather than a single day's position. Don't panic if a keyword drops or disappears from the results on a given day. Rankings fluctuate, so look at the overall trend instead.</p>
<h2>4. Traffic is growing on the pages that bring in business</h2>
<p>Total traffic can climb while your service and location pages go nowhere. What you want to see is growth on the pages that actually generate leads. Determine what these pages are and watch their traffic specifically, as opposed to things like blog posts that can garner traffic without improving leads.</p>
<p><b>Where to check:</b> Google Analytics and Search Console's page-level data. Filter to organic search to isolate SEO impact.</p>
<h2>5. You're showing up more, and getting more clicks</h2>
<p>Impressions show how often you appear in search results. Click-through rate shows whether people choose you. Rising impressions with a steady or improving click-through rate is a strong sign your visibility is growing and your listings are compelling.</p>
<p><b>Where to check:</b> Search Console's Performance report for impressions, clicks, and CTR. For paid, check impression share and CTR in your ad platform.</p>
<h2>6. You're gaining ground in the map results</h2>
<p>For local businesses, the map pack is often where the decision happens. More visibility in the map results can lead to more calls, direction requests, and website visits from your Google Business Profile.</p>
<p><b>Where to check:</b> Your Google Business Profile performance data, plus a local rank tracker to see map position across your service area.</p>
<h2>7. More people are searching for you by name</h2>
<p>When people encounter your business in search results, an ad, a review, or an AI answer, they may come back later by searching for your business by name. Growth in branded searches can be a sign that more people are becoming aware of your business and actively looking for you.</p>
<p><b>Where to check:</b> Search Console, filtering queries to include your business name. Compare against the previous period and the same time last year.</p>
<h2>8. AI platforms are mentioning you and sending visitors</h2>
<p>More people now use tools like ChatGPT, Claude, and Google's AI features to research and choose businesses. Being cited in those answers is an emerging sign of authority, and some of it shows up as measurable traffic.</p>
<p><b>Where to check:</b></p>
<ul>
<li><b>Google Analytics:</b> open your traffic acquisition report, switch the view to source or medium, and look for referrals from chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, gemini.google.com, copilot.microsoft.com, and similar. The exact menu labels shift occasionally, but the source/medium view is the one to use.</li>
<li><b>Manual spot checks:</b> pick a set of questions your customers actually ask, run them periodically in a few AI tools, and note whether your business is mentioned or cited. This is also the best way to check Google's own AI answers, since those visits generally show up as regular Google search traffic.</li>
</ul>
<p>A caution: this area is still young, and the numbers are small for most sites. Treat it as a trend to watch rather than a precise score.</p>
<h2>9. Visitors are more engaged</h2>
<p>Good SEO and <a href="/services/pay-per-click/">well-targeted PPC</a> should bring the right people to pages that answer their questions and make it easy to take the next step. That might mean reading about a service, viewing multiple pages, clicking to call, submitting a form, or booking an appointment.</p>
<p><b>Where to check:</b> Google Analytics for engagement rate, average engagement time, landing-page performance, and the actions visitors take on your site.</p>
<h2>10. You're earning more reviews and building trust</h2>
<p>New reviews demonstrate that not only are you getting leads, but also that your customers can easily find you online to leave the review. A steady flow of recent, positive reviews is a sign your online presence is working on multiple fronts.</p>
<p><b>Where to check:</b> Google Business Profile and other review sites you rely on. Track review count, average rating, and recency, and whether you're responding.</p>
<h2>Results come from the whole picture</h2>
<p>No single number tells you whether your marketing is working. Rankings can climb without bringing in a single lead. Ad spend can look efficient while sending the wrong people to your site. The real story shows up when you connect the dots: visibility, traffic, conversions, and revenue, all measured against where you started.</p>
