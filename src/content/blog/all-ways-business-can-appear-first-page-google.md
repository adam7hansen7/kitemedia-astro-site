<p>When business owners talk about “getting on the first page of Google,” they’re often thinking about one thing: ranking #1 in the organic search results.</p>
<p>But Google’s first page just doesn’t work that way anymore.</p>
<p>Today, the first page of Google is made up of many different sections: ads, maps, AI-generated answers, and more. While this can sometimes be overwhelming for business owners, the good news is that there’s more than one way for your business to show up. Understanding these options can help you make smarter marketing decisions and avoid wasting money chasing the wrong strategy.</p>
<p>Check out the following example of how Kite Media is able to secure four different features on the front page, dominating the search results:</p>
<p><img alt="A google SERP showing Kite Media occupying four different features on the front page of Google" height="1024" loading="lazy" src="/images/2026/01/Kitemediasearchresults-1024x1024.webp" width="1024"/></p>
<h2>The First Page of Google Isn’t One Static Thing</h2>
<p>Before we break down the different placements, it’s important to understand one key idea: There is no single, fixed version of the first page of Google. Not all of these features will show up for every keyword, or even for every search for the same keyword.</p>
<p>What someone sees can change based on:</p>
<ul>
<li>Their location (even within the same city)</li>
<li>Whether they’re on mobile or desktop</li>
<li>Their search history and personalization</li>
<li>The exact wording of the search</li>
<li>The time of day</li>
<li>Live ad auctions happening in real time</li>
</ul>
<p>That’s why two people (or the same person on different devices or at different times) can search the same phrase and see different results, and why rankings are better measured over time, not from one screenshot or quick search.</p>
<p>With that in mind, let’s look at the main ways businesses can appear on page one.</p>
<h2>Google’s AI Overviews (The Newest First-Page Feature)</h2>
<p>In many searches, Google now displays AI-generated summaries at the very top of the results. These overviews are designed to quickly answer questions by pulling information from multiple trusted sources.</p>
<p>For businesses, this works differently than traditional rankings since you don’t “rank” in the AI Overview. Instead, a few sites are sourced in the answer. The value of appearing in the AI overview also depends greatly on the search intent.</p>
<p>For informational searches, such as “How do I clean pet urine out of carpet?”, AI Overviews are designed to educate. A business may be referenced or mentioned as a source, which can help with brand awareness and authority, but these searches typically do not generate immediate leads. The user is looking for answers, not a service provider.</p>
<p>For commercial and local searches, such as “Who is the best carpet cleaning company in Logan?”, AI Overviews can play a much bigger role in lead generation. Appearing in these overviews can drive clicks and calls, making these mentions much more valuable.</p>
<p>Due to their AI nature, AI overviews are always changing, and appearing (or not appearing) in one search isn’t reflective overall of your business’s ability to appear in other searches. Every AI overview appearance is a win for a business, though, since it signals Google's trust in your website and brand.</p>
<h2>Local Services Ads (LSAs)</h2>
<p><a href="/everything-you-need-to-know-about-google-local-service-ads/">Local Services Ads</a> are a valuable type of advertising because they often appear above everything else on the page for certain service industries. These ads are sometimes called “Google Guaranteed” ads.</p>
<p>Key characteristics:</p>
<ul>
<li>Available for many home and professional services</li>
<li>Businesses go through a verification process</li>
<li>Pricing is typically pay-per-lead, not pay-per-click</li>
<li>Visibility is influenced by reviews, responsiveness, and proximity</li>
</ul>
<p>LSAs can be extremely powerful, but they also offer less control than traditional PPC ads and aren’t available to every business or market. We’ve gotten great results with LSAs for many of our clients, and they are definitely worth exploring if they are available for your market.</p>
<h2>Pay-Per-Click (PPC) Search Ads</h2>
<p>These are the text ads that often appear at the top and bottom of Google’s search results. They are marked with a “sponsored” label to separate them from the organic results.</p>
<p>PPC ads work through a live auction system that considers:</p>
<ul>
<li>Your bid</li>
<li>Ad relevance</li>
<li>Landing page quality</li>
<li>Competition at that moment</li>
</ul>
<p>Because of this, PPC visibility can change from search to search, even within the same day. The biggest advantage of PPC is speed—you can appear on page one almost immediately—but visibility stops when the ads stop running. PPC ads also give you a lot of control over your ad text and what keywords you show up for, powerful tools in the hands of an <a href="/services/pay-per-click/">experienced PPC marketer</a>.</p>
<h2>Google Business Profile &amp; the Map Pack</h2>
<p>The map section, often called the map pack, is one of the most valuable areas on the first page for local businesses. The map pack can vary from search to search but often has two sections:</p>
<h3>Organic Map Pack Listings</h3>
<p>These are the three (sometimes fewer) <a href="/common-gbp-mistakes/">Google Business Profiles</a> that appear with the map.</p>
<p>Visibility is influenced by:</p>
<ul>
<li>Proximity to the searcher</li>
<li>Relevance to the search</li>
<li>Prominence (reviews, activity, authority)</li>
</ul>
<p>For many service businesses, the map pack drives some of the highest-intent traffic available on Google. They offer customers the option to call you or get directions to your business with a single click and showcase your reviews and other business information.</p>
<h3>Ads in the Map Pack</h3>
<p>Google also allows paid ads within the map results, shown as sponsored results. These are tied to Google Ads and your Google Business Profile. Marketers don't have a lot of control over these ads, but they are valuable to businesses when they do appear. A big advantage of these ads is that your business can show up in the organic map pack and as a sponsored ad in the map pack, giving you two listings and a lot of prominence. Where map pack competition is high, a map ad gives you the opportunity to show up where you otherwise might not have.</p>
<h2>Organic Search Results (Traditional SEO)</h2>
<p>These are the familiar blue links that appear below ads and maps. This is often what people think of when they think of Google search results and <a href="/services/search-engine-optimization/">SEO</a>. While they are usually below the sponsored listings, they still hold a lot of value. A lot of customers scroll right past any sponsored results and look for the organic results. And of course, it’s beneficial to appear multiple times on a page, such as in the ads and the organic search results. This reinforces your business as a reputable and popular option.</p>
<p><a href="/why-seo-marathon-not-sprint/">SEO is a long-term strategy</a>, but it supports nearly every other Google placement, from AI Overviews to maps to ad performance. Rankings naturally fluctuate, which is normal and expected, especially in competitive markets, but tracking your results over time can give you a clearer view of your organic standing.</p>
<h2>Google Knowledge Panels</h2>
<p>On desktop searches, some businesses appear in a Knowledge Panel on the right-hand side of the results. Knowledge Panels usually appear for branded searches and are built from strong entity signals, consistent information across the web, and alignment between your website and Google Business Profile. While they don’t usually drive discovery, they play an important role in trust and credibility.</p>
<h2>Additional First-Page Features</h2>
<p>Depending on the search, your business may also appear in:</p>
<ul>
<li>People Also Ask question boxes</li>
<li>Image results</li>
<li>Video results</li>
</ul>
<p>These placements can provide extra exposure, but for local service businesses, they’re best viewed as bonuses, not the core strategy.</p>
<h2>You Don’t Need to Be Everywhere to Win on Google</h2>
<p>A common mistake businesses make is trying to show up everywhere at once. The reality is that most businesses succeed by focusing on one or two high-impact placements that align with their goals, budget, and competition.</p>
<p>For example:</p>
<ul>
<li>New businesses often benefit from PPC and LSA ads</li>
<li>Established businesses may prioritize SEO and organic maps</li>
<li>Highly competitive markets may need a mix of PPC, map ads, and LSAs</li>
<li>If one area is not working well, shifting focus to another can help improve results</li>
</ul>
<h2>Final Thoughts: First-Page Visibility Is Strategic, Not Static</h2>
